---
title: Materials for laser cutting and engraving
---

A laser works by concentrating heat onto a tiny spot. That's why every material reacts differently: some cut with finished edges straight off the bed, some only accept surface engraving, and a few release corrosive or toxic gases and must never go into a machine. Here is how each material behaves in our Valencia workshop.

## Wood, plywood and MDF

One of the most versatile materials for laser work. It cuts and engraves with crisp detail, leaving a natural toasted edge and zero wood shavings.

- **Solid wood:** every plank has its own grain and density, so no two pieces engrave identically. Light, even-grained timbers give sharper contrast, and dense woods support deep relief (~1.5 mm on the Ron Barceló bottle stand).
- **Plywood (such as birch):** light, flat and stable — our go-to for openwork mandalas, coasters, fans, scale models and interlocking parts.
- **MDF:** completely smooth and uniform throughout, ideal for painted corporate letters and consistent engraving tones.

We use it for names, boxes, stands, boards, decoration, falla crests and wedding favours. See [laser cutting and engraving on wood](/en/laser-cutting-engraving-wood/).

## Acrylic (PMMA)

Acrylic is the cleanest plastic to cut with a CO2 laser. The beam melts and seals the edge as it cuts, leaving a **bright, flame-polished finish** with no manual sanding. Surface engraving turns crisp frosted white, creating strong contrast on clear, black or coloured panels.

We cut and engrave clear, coloured, matte and mirror acrylic across thicknesses for shop signs, corporate letters, scale models, trophies, black cards and displays. See [acrylic and glass](/en/laser-cutting-engraving-acrylic-glass/).

## Glass and crystal

Glass is **engraved, not laser cut**: the beam creates a microscopic surface frost that feels smooth, looks crisp white and survives repeated washing. It is the classic finish for wedding champagne flutes, restaurant wine glasses, bottles, jugs and awards.

Because glass composition and tempering vary between manufacturers, frosting tone can differ slightly from one glass to another; we always check your pieces before starting.

## Natural leather

Natural leather engraves by darkening the hide with gentle relief — no inks or hot-stamping dies — and also cuts cleanly into intricate shapes or stitch-ready labels.

- **Contrast by hide colour:** tan, cognac and light hides give strong dark contrast; on black or very dark leather the mark reads mainly through subtle relief and matte texture.
- **Watch out for synthetic leather:** many faux leathers contain PVC (see below), so we always verify composition before accepting an item.

See [leather engraving and cutting](/en/laser-engraving-leather/).

## Metal: stainless steel, aluminium, brass and precious metals

On metal, the standard job is **permanent marking and engraving**, matching CO2 or fibre lasers to the alloy:

- **Anodised or lacquered aluminium (CO2 or fibre):** strips away the coloured surface layer to expose bright aluminium beneath — crisp contrast for rating plates, barcodes and QR codes.
- **Stainless steel, brass, copper, titanium, sterling silver and gold (fibre):** marks bare metal directly; on stainless steel, laser annealing produces a dark, surface-level mark without removing metal or reducing corrosion resistance.

If you also need sheet metal cut to size, let us know the exact alloy and thickness so we can review it. See [metal laser engraving](/en/laser-engraving-metal-stainless-steel/).

## Other laser-friendly materials

- **Card and paper:** ultra-fine die-free cutting for invitations, architectural models and packaging prototypes.
- **Cork:** coasters, lids and favours with warm high-contrast engraving.
- **Fabric and felt:** synthetic fibres seal along the cut line so edges don't fray.
- **Stone, slate and ceramic:** surface engraving (not cutting) with a light, distinctive mark on dark tones.

## Materials we never laser

For operator safety, optics care and finish quality, we turn down:

- **PVC and vinyl:** when heated they release toxic chlorine gas that corrodes mirrors, lenses and rails. Many faux leathers and banners are PVC.
- **Polycarbonate:** instead of cutting cleanly, it melts, yellows and gives off dense smoke. For tough clear plastic parts, acrylic (PMMA) is the right choice.
- **Unknown coatings:** if a coated sheet or painted plastic has no known composition, confirm its spec sheet first.

If you have a material or finished item and aren't sure whether it's safe, **send us a photo and whatever you know about it** (name, composition or thickness) and we'll confirm before you bring it in.
