---
title: Materials for laser cutting and engraving
---

The laser works by heating the material at a very small spot. That's why the result depends so much on the material: some cut cleanly, some engrave beautifully but don't cut, and a few give off gases that should never go into a machine. Here is a tour of the most common ones.

## Wood, plywood and MDF

The most rewarding material. It cuts and engraves in fine detail, and the laser leaves a toasted tone that becomes part of the finish.

- **Solid wood:** every board has its own grain, so no two engravings come out identical. Light woods give more contrast.
- **Plywood:** light and stable, ideal for parts that slot together, models and decoration.
- **MDF:** a smooth, even surface, perfect for painting afterwards or for very regular engravings.

We use it for names, boxes, stands, boards, decoration and gifts. More in [laser cutting and engraving on wood](/en/laser-cutting-engraving-wood/).

## Acrylic

Acrylic (PMMA) is one of the materials that responds best to the laser: the cut leaves a polished, glass-like edge, and engraving comes out white and frosted. It comes clear, in colours, mirrored and in matt finishes.

It's used for displays, signs, corporate letters, models, trophies and signage. More in [acrylic and glass](/en/laser-cutting-engraving-acrylic-glass/).

## Glass

Glass is **engraved**, not laser cut: the beam creates a very fine white frosting on the surface. It's the classic finish for wine glasses, bottles, jugs, trophies and commemorative plaques.

## Leather

Natural leather engraves with a sunken, darker relief that looks very elegant, and it can also be cut into small pieces. Wallets, belts, keyrings, cases and labels are the typical jobs. Watch out for synthetic leathers: many are PVC (see below). More in [leather engraving](/en/laser-engraving-leather/).

## Metal

On metal the usual thing is to **mark or engrave** the surface: stainless steel, anodised aluminium, brass… The result is a sharp, permanent mark, ideal for plaques, tools, industrial parts, tags and jewellery. If you need a part cut out of metal, tell us the material and thickness and we'll tell you how to make it. More in [metal engraving](/en/laser-engraving-metal-stainless-steel/).

## Other materials that work well

- **Card and paper:** very fine cuts for invitations, models and prototypes.
- **Cork:** coasters, boards and details with a warm engraving.
- **Fabric and felt:** cuts without fraying on many synthetic fibres, because the laser seals the edge.
- **Stone, slate and ceramic:** engraved (not cut) with a light, striking finish.

## Materials that are not lasered

For safety and quality, there are materials a serious laser workshop turns down:

- **PVC and vinyl:** when heated they release chlorine, which is toxic and corrodes the machine. Many synthetic and faux leathers are PVC.
- **Polycarbonate:** it melts and yellows instead of cutting cleanly.
- **Materials with unknown coatings:** if nobody knows what it is, ask for its data sheet first.

If you have a material and don't know whether it works, **bring it in or send us a photo with whatever you know about it** (brand, name, thickness) and we'll tell you whether it can be done.
