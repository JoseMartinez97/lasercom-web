title: Laser cutting or engraving
---

When someone asks us for "something with the laser", they almost always mean one of three processes: **cutting**, **engraving** or **marking**. All three rely on focused laser beams, but what changes is the laser source, how much energy reaches the material and whether material is removed or just transformed at the surface.

## Laser cutting: the shape comes out of the sheet

When cutting, the beam travels right through the material along a closed path. The result is a loose piece with the exact shape of your drawing: a cut-out name, a mandala, a corporate letter, a falla crest or the walls of a scale model.

- **What you see:** a crisp, accurate contour. On wood the cut edge turns a warm dark toasted brown; on acrylic the edge comes out smooth and glossy without manual polishing.
- **What it needs:** a closed-line drawing (**vector**). The machine follows every contour path at 1:1 real scale.
- **Kerf and tolerances:** the beam itself has a tiny width (kerf), usually a few tenths of a millimetre. For standalone parts it makes no practical difference, but for interlocking boxes or scale models we size slots to the measured sheet thickness.
- **What it's for:** silhouettes, corporate lettering, openwork coasters, templates, technical gaskets, decoration and models.

## Laser engraving: the design sits in the surface

When engraving, the beam does not cut through: it vaporises or burns a thin top layer to create sunken relief. You can engrave a filled area (a logo, text or photograph) or trace a fine single-line outline.

- **What you see:** a textured, recessed design. On wood it shows in toasted tones (and on solid timber can reach deep relief, such as the ~1.5 mm depth on the Ron Barceló 30th anniversary stand); on glass and acrylic it turns bright frosted white; on natural leather, darker and sunken.
- **What it needs:** a vector logo or text for razor-sharp edges, or a high-resolution, well-lit photograph when engraving a portrait.
- **Filled engraving vs. line scoring:** filling solid shapes requires the head to sweep line by line (*raster*), whereas scoring a thin vector line runs much faster.
- **What it's for:** personalising gifts, boxes, boards, wine and champagne glasses, bottles, wallets, belts or finished musical instruments.

## Laser marking: permanent contrast without removing material

Marking alters the colour or top oxide layer of metal while removing virtually no material and leaving part strength intact. It is the standard process on stainless steel, brass, copper, silver, gold and anodised aluminium.

- **Laser annealing on stainless steel:** heats the metal locally to create a dark, high-contrast mark flush with the surface while preserving corrosion resistance.
- **Anodised or coated aluminium marking:** the beam strips away only the dyed or lacquered coating to expose bright bare aluminium underneath — ideal for high-contrast plates and scannable codes.
- **What it's for:** sequential serial numbers, QR and barcodes, inventory plates, tools, jewellery and metal business cards.

## Cutting and engraving on the same piece

Many jobs combine both in a single run on the bed: a cut-out keyring with an engraved name, a box lid engraved and cut to size, a wedding place card or an office door plate with an engraved logo and cut border. In your file, simply put cut lines and engraving fills on separate layers or colours.

## What affects the quote?

Because every job is quoted to measure (no flat menu pricing), cost depends mainly on machine time, file prep and material:

- **Engraved area:** large solid fills take more head passes than fine outlines or small lettering.
- **Cut length and thickness:** an intricate mandala with dozens of inner holes or a thick panel requires more cutting time than a simple rectangle.
- **Batch size and setup:** preparing and proofing artwork takes a fixed setup effort, so unit cost drops on larger runs.

When asking for a quote, sharing the approximate dimensions, material and quantity helps us reply quickly and accurately.

## How to tell which one you need

- Do you want a **loose cut-out shape**? Cutting.
- Do you want to **personalise an object** in wood, glass, leather or acrylic? Engraving.
- Do you need to **mark metal parts or jewellery** permanently? Marking.
- Still unsure? Send us a photo or rough sketch of what you have in mind and we'll advise you.
