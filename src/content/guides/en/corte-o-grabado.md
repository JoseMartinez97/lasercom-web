title: Laser cutting or engraving
---

When someone asks us for "something with the laser", they almost always mean one of three things. All three use the same beam; what changes is how much energy reaches the material and how the head moves.

## Laser cutting: the shape comes out of the sheet

When cutting, the beam goes right through the material along a line. The result is a loose piece with the exact shape of the drawing: a cut-out name, a mandala, a corporate letter or a part for a scale model.

- **What you see:** a clean outline. On wood the edge is toasted a characteristic dark brown; on acrylic the edge comes out polished and glossy.
- **What it needs:** a line drawing (vector). The machine follows the line, so it must be closed and at real size.
- **What it's for:** silhouettes, letters, parts that fit together, templates, decoration and models.

## Laser engraving: the design stays on the surface

When engraving, the beam doesn't go through: it burns or vaporises a thin layer of the surface. You can engrave a filled area (a logo, text, even a photo) or just a fine line.

- **What you see:** a design set slightly into the surface. On wood it comes out in toasted tones; on glass and acrylic it turns white and frosted; on leather, darker and sunken.
- **What it needs:** a logo or text as a vector or, for photographs, a good-resolution image.
- **What it's for:** personalising gifts, plaques, boxes, glasses, wallets, boards or any object that already exists.

## Laser marking: a change of colour without removing material

Marking changes the colour of the surface while removing hardly any material. It is the usual choice on metal and industrial parts: serial numbers, QR codes, barcodes or logos that must last and stay readable without damaging the part.

- **What you see:** a sharp, permanent mark, usually dark, flush with the surface.
- **What it's for:** traceability, identifying parts and tools, rating plates.

If that's what you need, see our [industrial laser marking](/en/industrial-laser-marking/).

## Cutting and engraving on the same piece

Many jobs combine both in one pass through the machine: a cut-out keyring with an engraved name, a box with an engraved lid, a sign with an engraved logo and a cut outline. In the file you only need to separate what is cut from what is engraved (see below how).

## What affects the price?

In a laser workshop the cost depends mostly on machine time and material:

- **Engraving:** the larger and more filled the engraved area, the longer it takes. A fine line engraves much faster than a solid logo of the same size.
- **Cutting:** the total length of the lines and the thickness of the material.
- **Units:** setting up a job takes a fixed time, so in runs the price per piece usually goes down.

That's why, when you ask for a quote, it helps us to know the approximate size, the material and how many units you need.

## How to tell which one you need

- Do you want a **loose piece with a shape**? Cutting.
- Do you want to **personalise an object** you have or will buy? Engraving.
- Do you need to **identify metal parts** permanently? Marking.
- Not sure? Send us a photo or a sketch of what you have in mind and we'll tell you how to make it.
